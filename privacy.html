Privacy Policy

Last updated 23 September 2026 · applies to Nonogram: Cozy Picture Cross for iPhone and iPad.

This policy describes what the app collects and who handles it. It was written by going through the app's own source code and its shipping privacy manifest, not from a template — where a section says the app does not do something, that is because there is no code in it that does.

Who is responsible

DevOrbit Labs is the data controller for the processing described here. You can reach a person at halimozturk@windowslive.com.

What stays on your device

Your game itself — which puzzles you have solved, the board you are in the middle of, your coins, your streak, your gallery, your settings — is written to storage on your device and mirrored to your own iCloud account so that it reaches your other devices. That mirror uses Apple's key-value storage inside your personal iCloud container. We have no access to it and no server of our own that it is copied to.

There is no account to create. The app never asks for your name, your email address, your phone number or your date of birth, and there is nowhere in it to type them.

The app includes four third-party components, each doing one job.

Things the app does not collect

No name, email address, phone number, postal address or contacts. No precise location and no location permission of any kind. No photos, microphone or camera access. No health or fitness data. No financial information — the coins in the game are game pieces, and Apple handles payment without passing us your card details. No free text, because there is nowhere in the app to write any. No browsing or search history, because the app has no search and no browser.

Advertising and the tracking prompt

The game is supported by ads. Google's consent flow runs first where local law requires it, and on iOS the system's App Tracking Transparency prompt follows, asking whether the app may use your advertising identifier.

Saying no is a real choice and costs you nothing in the game. You will still see ads; they will simply be less relevant, and your advertising identifier is not used to link you across other apps and websites. You can change the answer at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking on your device.

Ads are served by Google. Google's own handling of the data is described in its privacy and terms for partner sites.

Game Center

If you are signed in to Game Center, achievements and leaderboard entries are handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy. The app does not store a Game Center identifier of its own.

Children

The app is rated 4+, but it is a general-audience game and is not directed at children under 13. It does not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has provided information through the app, write to the address above and it will be deleted.

Where the data goes

The processors named above operate in the United States and in the European Union. Transfers out of the EEA and the UK rest on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, which each of those processors has in place.

How long it is kept

Usage events are kept by Mixpanel for as long as they are useful for product analysis and are then deleted on that service's schedule. Purchase records are kept by RevenueCat while your purchases remain valid and for as long as tax and accounting law requires afterwards. The game data on your device and in your iCloud is kept until you delete the app or clear it yourself.

Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask for a copy of what is held about you, ask for it to be corrected, ask for it to be deleted, or object to it being processed. If you are in the EEA or the UK, those rights come from the GDPR; if you are in California, from the CCPA/CPRA. Write to halimozturk@windowslive.com and you will get an answer within 30 days.

Two of these you can exercise yourself, immediately: turn off tracking in Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking, and delete the app to remove its data from your device. You may also reset your advertising identifier in the same Settings section at any time.

We do not sell personal information for money. We should be straightforward about the rest: when you allow tracking, your advertising identifier goes to our advertising partner so that the ads can be personalised — and under the CCPA that counts as sharing for cross-context behavioural advertising. Declining the tracking prompt, or turning it off later in Settings, is how you opt out of it, and the game is unchanged either way.

Changes

If what the app collects changes, this page is rewritten from the code again and the date at the top changes with it. Material changes will also be noted in the App Store release notes for the version that makes them.
